April 15, 2024

Subject: A Letter of Gratitude

The VC, Pro VCs, Registrar, Deans, Chairmen, Provost, Career Development center, and Student Council

A.O.A

I hope you all had a great time with your families on the occasion of Eid al-Fitr. First of all, by the grace of God and secondly with your administration’s kind support, I also had the happiest Eids. I don’t have words to express my thanks to the University’s administration for providing us with an opportunity to feel at home despite being thousands of miles away from our families—gratitude for everything.

It all started on 9th April, the 29th day of Ramadan. A day earlier, all the outstation students living in various external hostels were called to the internal hostel at the provost’s instructions to ensure foolproof security. There could also be some other reasons that I would let you comprehend. I was told that the Sehri on the last day of Ramadan was arranged by the hostel department for out-of-province and international students, or, it would be better to say, Sri Lankan students. The last iftar was no match compared to a royal dinner. It had been prepared by the experienced cooks with love and care, adding a pinch of salt, spices, water, tomatoes and onions on behalf of all the well-wishers. This was just the beginning, and the feast was just about to start. Let me tell you, all the shops, hotels and restaurants in the periphery of the university were closed. If it were not the hostel department and Career Development center, then we would have been starved to death.

With the announcement of Eid al-Fitr, our Eid began. On the morning of 10th April, after Eid prayers, the outstation students were gathered outside the university Masjid. Several administrative heads, teachers, and staff, along with their families, celebrated Eid with us. I never felt we were away from home, especially the Sri Lankan students, who were very happy to see the caring nature, as this was a new experience for them. They have been here for a couple of years, and they still don’t know how kind and caring we are. And on occasions of celebrations, we don’t have a match in the whole world. I think those who have international exposure would certainly agree with me. I am sure once the Sri Lankans are back home, they will be our delegates. The way they have been treated will bring more and more students to our university. I will also do the same thing when I am back in Gilgit.

Around 8:00 am, we were served hot tea with garma garam parathas, fried eggs, jam and halwa. We used to receive 1000 rupees as Eid al-Fitr, but this time the hostel department distributed 2000 rupees among the students, reflecting a 100 per cent increase, taking inflation into account. The lunch was arranged by someone I don’t know. He seemed like a big-hearted person who showered us with food from the very famous Hotel and Restaurant. My god, the dinner was delicious. I was delighted, and one can imagine it as a dream candlelight dinner with a loved one. Finally, the first day of Eid ended with a visit from a senior faculty member, who distributed gifts to students. The second and third days were no different from the first in terms of food, but unfortunately, we did not receive any gifts. Eid mily, eidi bi mili, maza dar khana bi to or kya chahiye…

I would not be able to thank every one of the worthy administrators who are always there, particularly for students outside of Sindh. But I can say thank you to our dear Vice Chancellor Sab, as he is the head of the institute. Sir, I don’t know where to start. You might remember me, if not by name, then by the picture you took last year when I was at your office. I wish I had that photo of you, but unfortunately, I was not lucky enough to have you in the frame. I came to your office with a group of outstation students to seek your permission to stay in the hostels during the Eid vacation. Before you were appointed VC, how the management used to facilitate the outstation students is a great mystery. This can only be unfolded by the senior faculty or the passed-out students, if and only if one wishes to know. But I think it would be a waste of time to try to solve that conundrum, as there are other goals to achieve for students’ well-being.

Well, before entering your office, I was nervous because of the whispers that you do not listen to students or even administrators. But when you hear about our concern, you cancel all the important meetings of the day within minutes. I did not say “apologies” back then, as it may have been hectic for you and the VC office secretary to reschedule all those critical meetings. You welcomed us with full zeal and zest, and at that moment, all the whispers proved wrong. You allow us to make ourselves comfortable around the sofa right in front of your seat. If I recall correctly, there were two other gentlemen with you, and they were also having a vital meeting, but you listened to our concern first with great patience.

You shared your experience studying at a university in Japan. You also talked about how you depicted a very soft image of our country to the Japanese and taught them the values of our society, particularly the way we treat our guests. I was impressed to learn that while studying, you can also learn Japanese in a very short period. Well, back to the permission minute issue: within minutes, you made it part of the policy to allow all out-of-Sindh-province and international students to remain in hostels, even for ten days, and ordered that the best available food and facilities be provided. Once again, thank you for that. When we were about to leave the office, you advised us to learn Sindhi, which would help us make good friends. I think I have successfully made a lifelong friend and will be learning Sindhi from that person. Till now, I have learned many words like budaiyo, khappa, maani hhania…

Moreover, last year there was still a different or rather, the old administration that treated us differently. Probably that set of administrators has different beliefs and another leader to follow. One of my teachers taught me this lesson a couple of years ago through his exceptional teaching method: that I don’t have the expertise to judge anyone. Given that, I would not be able to compare the pros and cons of the old and new ones as an undergraduate student. However, I intend to write this letter to thank the above-mentioned great personalities (as saluted) who have engraved “students: our priority” on the doors of their offices and mean it. Take the example of Provost Hostels. He might not know me, but I do remember his name, his words and his work. He is an example for those who want to excel at managing things. I think Dr Sab has a very calm nature and listens to students’ concerns regarding hostel issues. This Eid would not have been a treat without his keen interest in making students happy. Finally, thanks to Career Develpment Center, which directly interacts with students of every kind and assists with every small matter, such as providing stipends on time to secure jobs after graduation.

At last, it is time to thank the Pro VCs, the registrar, the deans, the chairpersons of the departments, the Provost, the CDC, and the SISC for a wonderful Eid, with a mix of great surprises for the outstation students, including the Sri Lankans. With Gratitude

P.S

Subject: Unreserved Apology for Sending Inappropriate Email to the Senior Management

Worthy Vice Chancellor and Esteemed Administration,

I am writing to extend my sincerest apologies for the recent incident in which I sent a lengthy email that caused harm. I want to express that my actions were solely my own, driven by personal instincts and judgments. I did not realise the impact of my actions at that time. I deeply regret the damage this has caused to the university’s reputation and to the continuous struggle of worthy Vice Chancellor Sab and Senior Management.

I want to assure you that I will take steps to address my issues and seek help to ensure such incidents never occur again.

Once again, I apologise unconditionally for my actions and the negative impact they have had on our university. I am truly sorry and will strive to uphold the values and standards that our university stands for.

With Sincerely Apologies,
